An [Element](/docs/reference/glossary/#term-element) is a node of the Model. There are four kinds, after the [C4 model](https://c4model.com):

| Kind | What it is | Nests in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Person](/docs/reference/glossary/#term-person) | A human or role that uses a System, such as a customer or a support agent. | Nothing: always top-level. |
| [System](/docs/reference/glossary/#term-system) | A named group of Services that together deliver a business capability. | Nothing: Systems don't nest. |
| [Service](/docs/reference/glossary/#term-service) | A deployable, addressable unit of software: the thing with a URL and Deployments. | A System, or nothing. |
| [Component](/docs/reference/glossary/#term-component) | A part inside a Service that isn't deployed on its own. | A Service. |

Nesting is strict: System › Service › Component. A Service may also stand outside any System.

## Declaring Elements

Elements are declared under the top-level keys `people`, `systems`, `services` and `components` of any Project file, keyed by id, with children under their parent. This is the Kitchen sink's Shop System, which has one of each kind of Service:

```yaml file="examples/kitchen-sink/archidocs/sink/model.yaml" region="systems"
```

Every Element has a `name` and can have a `description`, an `owner`, `tags` and `links`. On top of that:

- A **Service** has a `type`: `app` by default, `datastore` for a database or store, or `broker` for a message broker that async Relationships run through. Each is drawn differently.
- Services and Components have a `technology`, shown under their name.
- A System or Service can be **External**.

Every field is in the [Elements and Relationships reference](/docs/reference/model/model-yaml/).

## External Elements

An [External](/docs/reference/glossary/#term-external) System or Service runs outside the organisation, such as a payment provider or a card scheme. Its Owner is the Team accountable for the integration. An External Service has no declared Components ([`external-with-components`](/docs/reference/rules/external-with-components/)): you can't see inside it.

## Elements in more than one file

A child usually sits under its parent. When a Project splits its Model across files, a top-level Service can name its System with `in`, and a Component its Service:


## Levels

The viewer and [Views](/docs/concepts/views/) show the Model at a level:

- **C1**, the System landscape: People and Systems.
- **C2**, the Service landscape: Services, grouped in their Systems.
- **C3**: the Components inside a Service.

Each level is drawn from the same Elements. A diagram never adds an Element of its own.
