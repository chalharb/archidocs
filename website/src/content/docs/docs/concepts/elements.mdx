An [Element](/docs/reference/glossary/#term-element) is a node of the Model: a Person, a System, a Service or a Component. The four kinds come from the [C4 model](https://c4model.com), and they nest in one fixed order: System › Service › Component.

<Diagram view="kitchen-sink/sink:everything" alt="The Kitchen sink: a Customer, the Shop System with its Services and the API's Components, a Service outside any System, and External ones." />

## Four kinds

| Kind | What it is | Nests in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Person](/docs/reference/glossary/#term-person) | A human or role that uses a System, such as a customer or a support agent. | Nothing |
| [System](/docs/reference/glossary/#term-system) | A named group of Services that together deliver a business capability. | Nothing |
| [Service](/docs/reference/glossary/#term-service) | A deployable unit of software: the thing with a URL and Deployments. | A System, or nothing |
| [Component](/docs/reference/glossary/#term-component) | A part inside a Service that isn't deployed on its own. | A Service |

Elements are declared in any file of a Project, keyed by id, with children under their parent. This is the Kitchen sink's Shop System:

```yaml file="examples/kitchen-sink/archidocs/sink/model.yaml" region="systems"
```

Every Element has a name and an [Owner](/docs/reference/glossary/#term-owner), the one Team accountable for it. A Service also has a `type` that says what kind of software it is, such as a datastore or a message broker, and each type is drawn differently. The [Elements and Relationships reference](/docs/reference/model/model-yaml/) lists every field.

## Describing an Element

Besides its name and Owner, an Element can say more about itself. The viewer's Element panel shows each of these on its **Overview** tab:

- `description`: what the Element is or does, in [Markdown](https://commonmark.org/help/): emphasis, code, lists and links.
- `technology` (Services and Components): what it is built with, such as `Go` or `PostgreSQL`.
- `repository` (Systems, Services and Components): the URL of the repository its code lives in.
- `links`: other pages about it, such as a runbook or a dashboard, each with a `label`.
- `metadata`: [free-form facts](/docs/reference/glossary/#term-metadata) the Model has no field for, such as a tier or an on-call rota, as `key: value` pairs. Nothing in the Model reads them; they are there for people and agents to look up.

The Kitchen sink's API, in the Shop System above, uses all of them.

## Nesting

Nesting is strict. Systems don't nest in Systems, and Components don't nest in Components. A Service may also stand outside any System, as the Kitchen sink's Search does.

A child usually sits under its parent in the YAML. When a Project splits its Model across files, a Service declared at the top of another file can name its System with `in`, and a Component its Service.

## External Elements

An [External](/docs/reference/glossary/#term-external) System or Service runs outside your organization, such as a payment provider or a card scheme. Its Owner is the Team accountable for the integration.

You can't see inside an External Service, so it has no declared Components. `archidocs check` reports any as [`external-with-components`](/docs/reference/rules/external-with-components/).

## Levels

The viewer and [Views](/docs/concepts/views/) show the Model at one of three levels:

- **C1**, the System landscape: People and Systems.
- **C2**, the Service landscape: Services, grouped in their Systems.
- **C3**: the Components inside one Service.

Each level is drawn from the same Elements. A diagram never adds an Element of its own.

## Why it works this way

- **Fixed kinds.** Every Workspace has the same four kinds, so a C2 diagram means the same thing in every organization. An agent knows what a Service is without reading a custom schema.
- **Strict nesting gives the levels.** Because a Component is always inside a Service and a Service inside at most one System, the viewer can collapse any diagram to C1, C2 or C3 without asking how.
- **External is a flag, not a kind.** An External Service is still a Service: other Elements depend on it, and impact questions reach it. The flag only says that its inside is out of view.
- **`repository` is a field, not a link.** A link labeled "Repository" is a convention nothing checks, so an agent asking where a Service's code lives would have to guess the label. `metadata` takes the rest, so a Team's own facts never need a new field.
- **Ids don't depend on nesting.** Moving a Service into another System keeps its id, so nothing that refers to it breaks.
